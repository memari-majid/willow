## When anxiety is the main concern

Anxiety is not just feeling keyed up — it is usually a **prediction problem**. Your mind forecasts danger, your body responds as if the forecast were already true, and you may start avoiding situations that could disconfirm the fear.

Common patterns include:

- **Probability error** — "This will definitely go badly" when the realistic odds are lower.
- **Catastrophic error** — "If it goes wrong, I won't recover" when you have coped before.
- **Resource error** — "I can't handle it" when you have skills, people, or past evidence you are ignoring.

CBT does not ask you to pretend you feel calm. It helps you **test the thoughts driving the alarm** and choose responses that match reality more closely.

## What helps (techniques)

### Thought record for a specific anxious moment

Pick one recent situation — a meeting, message, bodily sensation, or "what if" spiral. Walk through situation → body → automatic thought → emotion (0–10) → evidence → a more accurate read → one small action. See the full [thought record guide](/wiki/techniques/thought-record).

### Probability and coping questions

Useful questions (in your own words, not as a checklist to rush):

- Out of 100 similar situations, how often did the feared outcome actually happen?
- What is the **realistic** worst case — and then what? Could you cope?
- What have you done before when things were hard?

### Worry postponement (for generalized worry)

If worry is free-floating rather than tied to one trigger, scheduling a short **worry time** later can reduce all-day rumination. Willow can help you set that up in chat.

## What Willow will not do here

- **Exposure hierarchies** for OCD, PTSD, panic disorder, or specific phobias without a clinician overseeing the plan. Willow can support motivation and debrief after exposure work you planned with a human therapist — not design new exposure protocols in chat.
- **Medication advice** — dosing, starting, or stopping belongs with a prescriber.
- **Diagnosis** — Willow can describe patterns; only a clinician can assess a disorder.

## When to involve a clinician

Seek professional support if anxiety severely limits work, relationships, or sleep; if you have panic attacks you cannot manage; if trauma is central; or if you are using alcohol or other substances to cope. Willow complements care — it does not replace it.
