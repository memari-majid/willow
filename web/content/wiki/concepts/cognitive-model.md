## The chain

Every CBT conversation rests on one idea:

**Situation (trigger) → Automatic thought → Emotion + body response + behavior**

- **Situations** can be external (a comment, a deadline) or internal (a memory, a body sensation, another thought).
- **Automatic thoughts** are fast, often unquestioned sentences in your mind ("They think I'm incompetent").
- **Emotions** have names and intensity (0–10 ratings make change visible).
- **Body responses** (tight chest, fatigue) are part of the picture, not distractions.
- **Behaviors** (avoiding, snapping, scrolling) usually follow the thought–feeling pair.

The model is not blaming you for having thoughts. It is a **map** for finding where to intervene.

## Doubt labels (core beliefs)

Under repeating automatic thoughts you may find **doubt labels** — harsh names you call yourself when self-doubt activates:

- Capability themes — "I'm not enough," "I can't handle this."
- Desirability themes — "I'm unlovable," "I'm too much."

Willow may gently hypothesize a theme when a pattern shows up. That is an invitation to explore, not a verdict about who you are.

## Examine, don't argue

The clinical stance is critical: **you never win an argument with a distressed mind by telling it it is wrong.** CBT uses guided discovery — evidence, alternative explanations, and experiments — so **you** reach a more accurate or helpful conclusion.

A thought can be:

- Partly true and still unhelpful.
- Emotionally convincing and still inaccurate.

Both can be true at once.

## How this shows up in chat

Willow will often:

1. Narrow to **one specific situation** instead of a global story.
2. Ask what went through your mind **in that moment**.
3. Rate an emotion 0–10 before and after looking at evidence.
4. End with **one** grounded question — not a menu of replies.
