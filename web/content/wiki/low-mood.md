## The low-mood loop

Low mood often creates a predictable cycle: you feel drained → you do less → you have fewer experiences of pleasure or accomplishment → you predict the future will feel just as flat → you do even less. The thoughts ("What's the point?", "I can't do anything right") are **real experiences**, but they are not always accurate forecasts.

CBT targets both sides of the loop:

- **Behavior** — gently re-introducing activity before motivation shows up.
- **Cognition** — examining the thoughts that justify withdrawal and all-or-nothing labels.

## Behavioral activation — start here

This is usually the first skill for low mood and inactivity:

1. **Track briefly** — note what you did in a few time blocks and rate mood 0–10. Patterns become visible quickly.
2. **List P and A** — activities that historically gave **P**leasure or a sense of **A**ccomplishment/mastery. Keep items small (a shower, a ten-minute walk, one email).
3. **Schedule one thing** — specific what, when, where. Not "exercise more" but "Tuesday 4pm, walk around the block."
4. **Predict then compare** — before you do it, predict your mood afterward. Depression often predicts "0"; the actual number is frequently higher — and that gap is therapeutic.

## Thought work when a situation sticks

When a specific setback dominates ("I failed the interview"), a [thought record](/wiki/techniques/thought-record) helps separate the event from global self-labels ("I'm a failure"). Willow examines thoughts with you; it does not argue you out of feeling bad.

## Important cautions

- If you have history of **manic or hypomanic episodes**, aggressive behavioral activation can be destabilizing — coordinate with a clinician.
- **Passive suicidal thoughts** belong in human care immediately; see [safety resources](/wiki/safety).
- Willow does not diagnose depression or recommend medication changes.
