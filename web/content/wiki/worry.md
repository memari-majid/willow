## When worry is the pattern

Some anxiety attaches to **one situation** (see [Anxiety](/wiki/anxiety)). **Worry** is often broader — repetitive "what if" loops that run in the background and feel urgent even when nothing is happening right now.

The cognitive behavioral therapy frame: worry can feel like problem-solving, but it often **rehearses catastrophes without producing a plan**.

## Skills that help

### Worry postponement

Pick a daily **worry time** (15–20 minutes). When worry intrudes outside that window, note the topic briefly and tell yourself you will return to it at the scheduled time. See the full [worry postponement guide](/wiki/techniques/worry-postponement).

### One worry on paper

Choose a single worry. Ask: **Is this a problem I can act on today?** If yes → one small action. If no → probability and coping questions from the [anxiety page](/wiki/anxiety).

### Thought record for a sticky worry

When one scenario dominates, use the [thought record](/wiki/techniques/thought-record) on that specific trigger.

## Scope

Willow will not diagnose generalized anxiety disorder or recommend medication. Persistent, impairing worry that has lasted months belongs with a clinician — Willow can support skills alongside care, not replace it.
