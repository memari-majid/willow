## Sleep and the mind

Poor sleep and low mood or anxiety often **feed each other**. At night, there are fewer distractions — automatic thoughts and worries can amplify. That does not mean you are "broken"; it means the [cognitive model](/wiki/concepts/cognitive-model) is running with less external input.

## aligned with cognitive behavioral therapy approaches (non-medical)

### Worry off the pillow

If bedtime is when rumination starts, try [worry postponement](/wiki/techniques/worry-postponement) — a scheduled worry time **earlier** in the evening, not in bed.

### Ground the body

Brief [grounding](/wiki/techniques/grounding) (5-4-3-2-1 senses) or slow breathing when the mind races — not to force sleep, but to reduce the fight with wakefulness.

### Stimulus control habits (behavioral)

- Use the bed mainly for sleep (not scrolling or problem-solving).
- If awake ~20 minutes, get up briefly, low light, boring activity, return when drowsy.
- Keep a consistent wake time even after a bad night.

### Thought work on sleep predictions

"I'll be useless tomorrow" is a common [catastrophizing](/wiki/distortions/catastrophizing) pattern. Examine evidence: times you functioned after poor sleep; what actually happened vs. what you feared.

## Scope

Willow **does not** recommend sleep medications, supplements, or dosing. Sleep apnea, chronic insomnia, and bipolar sleep disruption need medical or specialist assessment. This page is skill education only.
