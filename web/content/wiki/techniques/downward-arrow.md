## Purpose

A surface **automatic thought** ("I messed up the presentation") may sit on top of a **doubt label** ("I'm a failure"). The downward arrow helps you notice that deeper sentence — as a hypothesis, not a fact.

## How it works

After you name an automatic thought, ask variants of:

- "What would that mean if it were true?"
- "What's so bad about that?"
- "What does that say about you?"

Stop when you reach a **self-label** (doubt label) or when the chain plateaus or distress spikes.

## Pacing matters

This is **not** an interrogation. One question per turn. If you become overwhelmed, pause — validate, ground, and consider returning later. See [doubt labels](/wiki/concepts/doubt-labels).

## Often paired with

- [Thought record](/wiki/techniques/thought-record) step 6 (doubt label)
- [Socratic questioning](/wiki/techniques/socratic-questioning) for examining the label once surfaced

## Scope

Not for trauma narrative processing. Willow does not use this to push into traumatic material.

## Try it next

Start with one automatic thought that keeps returning — Willow will go one layer at a time.
