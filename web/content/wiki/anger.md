## What anger often tracks

Anger frequently follows a **violated rule** — often phrased as a "should" about yourself, others, or the world:

- "They **should** respect me."
- "This **shouldn't** be this hard."

The feeling is real. CBT asks whether the **should** is costing you more than the situation itself.

## What helps

### Down-regulate first when anger is hot

Before cognitive work: slow breathing, step away, brief walk. Anger narrows thinking; physiology first.

### Should → preference

"He should respect me" → "I'd **prefer** if he showed respect; when he doesn't, here are options I can take." See [should-statements](/wiki/distortions/should-statements).

### Assertive script

"I feel **X** when **Y** happens. I'd prefer **Z**." Practice in chat before a hard conversation.

### Thought record

For a specific incident, map situation → automatic thought → emotion → response with the [thought record](/wiki/techniques/thought-record).

## Scope

Willow does not counsel violence, revenge, or confrontation that could put you or others at risk. If anger leads to harm toward yourself or others, use [crisis resources](/wiki/safety) immediately.
